One canvas, many lenses

A mockup of the Map screen rebuilt as the app's single operating surface. The map stops being a tab and becomes the substrate everything runs on; a lens aims it and fills the console below. Venues and Guests ship first — vendors, lodging and day-of events land later as lens conformances, not as new navigation.

Companion to vowbase-ios-map-command-center-ux-spec.md Device iPhone 16 Pro · 393 × 852 pt Appearance Dark, matching the current build Type New York · SF Pro

Today the tab bar says Map / Venues / Guests, and the layer chips say Venues / Guests. Two controls, one taxonomy.

The lens rail replaces both. A lens is a focus, not a filter: the active layer goes full-size and tappable, everything else stays on the map at 70% scale and 55% opacity. You never lose sight of the people while you are choosing the place.

Lens 02 — Venues · peek detent

The map finally answers "so what?"

A venue is selected. The console names it, states its consequence, and offers the shortlist as a rail. The map is still 50% of the screen.

1

Context bar, 52 pt

Down from 80. The monogram shrinks to 36 pt inside a 44 pt target and the reclaimed space buys the wedding date and a search field. Past 365 days out it shows the date; inside a year it counts down.

2

Context layer — guests stay visible

70% scale, 55% opacity, no labels, not tappable, hidden from VoiceOver. Choosing Venues doesn't hide the people you're choosing for.

3

Focus layer — full-size, labelled on selection

Rose teardrop pins at 1.0 scale. Only the selected venue carries a name tag, so three pins never become three overlapping labels.

4

Controls ride the sheet

Fit, recenter and the quick-add button sit 12 pt above the console's top edge and travel with it. Nothing floats over a card any more — the current build's FAB overlaps the shortlist.

5

The impact readout

The single most valuable line on the screen, and absent today — travel is hardcoded to "Unavailable" while travelTimes sits unused in the API. Estimated results are labelled, never silently rounded. Tapping it jumps to Guests with this venue still selected.

6

Two facts, not four

The rail card drops location entirely — it's anchored to a pin you're already looking at. Name gets two lines. Nothing truncates.

7

Lens rail replaces the tab bar and the layer chips

Same glass capsule, same haptics, same sizing as the shipped tab bar — one new source of truth. Five slots maximum; a sixth lens moves the last slot to "More".

Lens 03 — Guests · peek detent

Same selection, flipped emphasis

Tapping the impact readout lands here. The venue stays selected; the guests it affects come forward, each cluster carrying its travel cost.

1

Clusters badged with travel

Drive times where a road route exists, a flight glyph where it doesn't. This is the "who does this hurt" view — the one thing a spreadsheet can't give you.

2

The selected venue survives the lens change

It drops to context opacity but keeps its selected ring, so the badges have a visible origin. Selection is global; the lens only decides who is loud.

3

Dead ends become tasks

"18 not counted" is a row that routes to the guests with no location. The current build's terminal "Unavailable" is never shown again — every failure state names its own fix.

4

The quick-add button is lens-aware

Tap adds the thing you're looking at — a guest here, a venue one lens over — and takes the lens tint so the action is legible before you press it. Long-press still opens the full panel.

Lens 01 — Overview · half detent

Where the other domains come in

Every layer at equal weight, nothing selected, the console stacked with modules. This is the extension point: a new domain ships one lens conformance and one module.

1

No focus layer, so no dimming

Overview is the only lens with nothing demoted. The camera fits everything and the map reads as a whole plan rather than one question.

2

Countdown, from data you already have

WeddingSummary.weddingDate is loaded and unused. Past a year out the module leads with the date; inside a year the day count takes the lead.

3

"Needs you" is derived, not authored

Five ordered rules over data already in memory — no backend work. Severity is a dot and a position, so urgency survives colour-blindness and greyscale.

4

The module contract is the framework

Each module declares id, urgency, size, destination and an empty state, then sorts itself. Budget burn, vendor payments due and day-of gaps slot in without touching the renderer.

Lens 02 — Venues · half detent

The Venues tab, absorbed

Drag the console up and it becomes the list you already built — same rows, same filters, same compare mode. Reached by a gesture instead of a tab, with the map still holding your place.

1

No screen title

The lens rail already says where you are. Deleting the eyebrow-plus-display-title block reclaims about 90 pt and puts two more venues above the fold.

2

The selection never leaves the map

Camera insets recompute from the live sheet height, so the selected venue stays framed as you drag. Dragging changes the detent and nothing else.

3

Tapping a row drops back to peek

The list explains the map; the map explains the list. That reciprocal move is the core interaction of the whole surface, and it's the reason these stopped being separate screens.

Dark-appearance values. Colour never carries meaning alone — each lens also owns a silhouette: teardrop, circle, diamond, rounded square, time glyph.

Type

New York for names and display, SF Pro for interface, tabular figures wherever durations and counts stack. Unchanged from VowbaseType.

Carried over unchanged

City-coarse guest geography, the glass capsule rail treatment, quick-add haptics, and every venue and guest detail screen. This proposal touches the map surface only.

Ship order
